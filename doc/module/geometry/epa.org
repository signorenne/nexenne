#+TITLE: EPA, penetration depth and normal
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil ^:nil
#+STARTUP: showall

* Purpose

=epa(a, b, simplex)= recovers how deep two overlapping convex shapes penetrate
and along which direction, picking up where [[file:gjk.org][GJK]] leaves off. Given GJK's overlap
simplex (the vertices carrying the origin of the Minkowski difference, which EPA
grows into a seed tetrahedron), the
Expanding Polytope Algorithm returns an =epa_result3<Real>=: the penetration
=normal= (unit, from B's surface toward A's), the =penetration_depth= (the
smallest translation that separates them), per-shape =contact_point_a= and
=contact_point_b=, and a =converged= flag. =Real= is deduced from the simplex.
Unlike the rest of the module it allocates, growing the polytope in a
=std::vector=; it is =noexcept=, so allocation failure terminates.

* The idea

GJK answers whether the origin is inside the Minkowski difference =A (-) B=. When
it is, the penetration depth is the distance from the origin to the *nearest
point on the boundary* of =A (-) B=, and the normal is the direction to it: that
is the shortest push that moves the origin out, hence the shortest translation
that separates the shapes. EPA finds that nearest boundary point by growing a
polytope inside =A (-) B=, starting from GJK's tetrahedron, always expanding the
face closest to the origin, until the closest face reaches the true surface.

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   boundary of A (-) B                 EPA expands the closest face outward
        ____                           toward the surface, step by step:
      /'    '\                            *  origin
     |   *o   |   o = origin             / \
      \ _   _/    nearest boundary      /   \   <- closest face, pushed out
        '--'      point => depth         \. ./      until it meets the surface
#+END_EXAMPLE

* How it works

** Seeding a tetrahedron from the GJK simplex

The signed-volumes [[file:gjk.org][GJK]] reports overlap with whatever simplex carries the origin,
which can be a single point, an edge, or a triangle, not always a full
tetrahedron. EPA first grows that seed to four affinely independent vertices: it
probes the world axes for a distinct second vertex, searches perpendicular to the
resulting edge for a third, and probes both sides of the triangle along its normal
for a fourth (taking the apex that reaches further off the plane). The origin,
having lain on the original lower simplex, ends up on a face or edge of the seed,
which the centroid-based orientation below handles. A simplex too degenerate to
grow (coincident or collinear supports) leaves EPA non-converged.

** The expanding polytope

EPA stores the polytope as a shared vertex list plus triangular faces indexing
into it. Each face caches its outward unit normal and its distance from the
origin. Seeded with the four tetrahedron vertices and four faces, the loop:

1. Picks the face nearest the origin (smallest distance).
2. Asks the shapes for a support point along that face's normal: the furthest
   point of =A (-) B= that way.
3. If the support point is no further out than the face (within a tolerance), the
   face is on the true surface: *converged*. Its normal is the penetration normal
   and its distance the depth.
4. Otherwise the surface is further out, so it *expands*: it adds the new vertex,
   deletes every face the vertex can see, and stitches the resulting hole (the
   horizon) to the new vertex with fresh faces. Then it repeats.

** Orienting faces, and why the origin cannot do it

Every face normal must point *outward* (away from the polytope interior) for the
closest-face search and the visibility test to mean what they say. The natural
trick, "flip the normal so it points away from the origin", fails exactly when
the penetration is shallow or the contact is symmetric, because then the origin
lies *on* a seed face and cannot say which side is out. Two axis-aligned cubes
meeting flush are the textbook case: GJK's tetrahedron has the origin sitting on
one of its edges, so two seed faces pass through the origin.

EPA here orients against the *centroid* of the seed tetrahedron instead. The
centroid is strictly inside the polytope and stays inside as the polytope only
grows, so it always resolves "which way is out", origin on a face or not.

** The winding invariant the horizon depends on

The horizon (the boundary of the deleted region) is found by walking the edges of
every visible face and cancelling each directed edge =(i, j)= against its reverse
=(j, i)=: an edge interior to the visible region appears once each way and
cancels, leaving only the true silhouette. This cancellation is correct *only if
every face winds the same way around its outward normal*. So when a face's normal
is flipped to point outward, two of its vertex indices are swapped as well, to
keep the winding consistent. Skipping that swap silently corrupts the polytope
and EPA never converges, which is the subtle failure mode this implementation
fixes.

** Contact points

On convergence, the origin's projection onto the closest face is expressed in
that face's [[file:triangle.org][barycentric coordinates]] (a Gram-matrix solve). Those same weights blend
the three stored per-shape support points into the world-space contact points on
A and on B, the deepest points of the contact.

* Complexity

| quantity          | cost                                                      |
|-------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------|
| support queries   | one per iteration, default cap 32                         |
| per iteration     | linear in the current face count (closest-face scan)      |
| memory            | the polytope, in std::vector (the one allocating routine) |

* When to use it

- After [[file:gjk.org][GJK]] reports an overlap, to turn the yes/no answer into the contact data a
  solver needs: depth, normal, and contact points. GJK and EPA are the standard
  pair for convex penetration.
- Pass GJK's terminal simplex straight through, whatever its size: EPA grows a
  point, edge, or triangle seed into a tetrahedron itself. Only a degenerate,
  non-expandable seed yields \c converged == false.
- For two analytic primitives with a closed-form penetration (sphere-sphere,
  AABB-AABB), prefer that: it is exact and cheaper. EPA is for general convex
  shapes, especially [[file:convex_hull.org][hulls]].
- The \c converged flag is part of the contract: on a degenerate input or if the
  iteration cap is hit, the best-known face is still returned but \c converged is
  \c false, so the caller can fall back.

* Usage

#+BEGIN_SRC cpp
namespace geo = nexenne::geometry;

auto const g = geo::gjk(a, b, center_b - center_a);
if (g.overlap) {
  auto const e = geo::epa(a, b, g.simplex);
  if (e.converged) {
    // Separate the shapes: move b by e.penetration_depth along e.normal.
    // e.contact_point_a / e.contact_point_b are the world-space contacts.
  }
}
#+END_SRC
