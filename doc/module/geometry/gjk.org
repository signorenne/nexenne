#+TITLE: GJK, convex overlap and distance
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil ^:nil
#+STARTUP: showall

* Purpose

=gjk(a, b, direction)= reports whether two convex shapes overlap and, when they
do not, how far apart they are, asking each shape only for its [[file:support.org][support]] function.
It returns a =gjk_result3<Real>=: an =overlap= flag; the =distance= and the
closest point on each shape (=closest_a=, =closest_b=) when they are apart; the
terminal =simplex= ([[file:epa.org][EPA]] grows it into the tetrahedron it needs for penetration
depth); and the =iterations= used. It allocates nothing (the simplex is a fixed
four-vertex array) and is =constexpr= and =noexcept=. =Real= is deduced from the
seed direction, so you write =gjk(a, b, dir)=, not =gjk<float>(a, b, dir)=.

* The idea

Two shapes A and B overlap if and only if they share a point, that is, if some
=pa in A= equals some =pb in B=, i.e. =pa - pb = 0=. The set of all differences
=pa - pb= is the *Minkowski difference* =A (-) B=. So the whole question collapses
to the origin's relationship to one convex set:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  A and B overlap     <=>   the origin lies inside  A (-) B
  distance(A, B)       =    distance from the origin to  A (-) B
#+END_EXAMPLE

=A (-) B= is itself convex, and we never build it explicitly. We only need to
probe it, and one probe suffices: its support function is
=support(A (-) B, d) = support(A, d) - support(B, -d)=. GJK uses that probe to
grow a small simplex (1 to 4 vertices) inside =A (-) B= and walk it toward the
origin, until either the simplex carries the origin (overlap) or a probe proves
the origin is outside and the closest point stops moving (separated, and the
closest point is the separation vector). This is the insight of Gilbert, Johnson,
and Keerthi (1988): collision and distance need no faces, no edges, only the
support mapping.

* How it works

** The Minkowski difference and the origin

If A and B touch, their difference set straddles the origin; if they are apart,
the whole difference set sits off to one side, and the point of =A (-) B= nearest
the origin is the separation.

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   two disks, apart            their Minkowski difference A (-) B

      A     B                         . . .
    (   ) (   )                     .       .       origin x is OUTSIDE; the
                          ===>     .    o    .      nearest point v of the set
                                    .   x---v       to x is the separation, and
                                     . . .          |v| is the distance

   two disks overlapping       A (-) B contains the origin

      A                              . . .
    (  X  )  B                     .       .        origin x is INSIDE
       (  )            ===>       .    x    .        => the shapes overlap
                                   .       .
                                     . . .
#+END_EXAMPLE

** Growing the simplex toward the origin

A *simplex* in 3D is a point, a segment, a triangle, or a tetrahedron (1 to 4
vertices). GJK keeps a simplex whose vertices are Minkowski-difference points and
repeats:

1. Find =v=, the point of the current simplex closest to the origin, and reduce
   the simplex to the smallest sub-simplex that carries =v= (the signed-volumes
   subalgorithm below).
2. Search along =d = -v= (from the closest point toward the origin) for a new
   support point =w= of =A (-) B=.
3. If =w= does not pass the origin (=dot(w, d) < 0=), that direction *separates*
   the shapes: they cannot overlap. If =w= adds no progress (it repeats a vertex
   or cannot move the closest point nearer), the distance has *converged*. Either
   way, stop; otherwise add =w= and loop.

The overlap verdict is decided by whether a separating direction is *ever* found:
a single one proves the shapes are apart, so if the loop *converges* without
having seen one, they intersect. A *convergence* stop therefore never misjudges
overlap. The one exception is running out of iterations before either a
separating direction or a convergence stop: that proves nothing, so GJK stays
conservative there (see Termination) and reports overlap only when the terminal
simplex actually encloses the origin.

** The signed-volumes distance subalgorithm

The heart of each step is: given the simplex, find the closest point of its convex
hull to the origin, and the minimal sub-simplex (vertex, edge, triangle) that
carries it. This is the *signed-volumes* method of Montanari, Petrinic and
Barbieri (2017), which decides the sub-simplex by comparing the signs of the
barycentric cofactors rather than forming and clamping a projection, so it stays
accurate to machine precision and never multiplies two tiny quantities. It
recurses by dimension:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  S3D (tetrahedron a,b,c,d):
    cofactors C_i = signed sub-volumes with the origin in vertex i's place,
    detM = C_a + C_b + C_c + C_d.
    all C_i share detM's sign  -> origin ENCLOSED: overlap, weights = C_i/detM
    otherwise                  -> for each face whose cofactor sign disagrees,
                                  recurse S2D and keep the nearest result
       (using cofactor signs, not face normals, keeps a near-flat tetrahedron
        from being mistaken for an enclosure: detM ~ 0 reduces it to a face)

  S2D (triangle a,b,c):  walk the vertex / edge / face Voronoi regions; the
                         interior case keeps all three, an edge keeps two, a
                         vertex keeps one

  S1D (segment a,b):     project the origin onto the line and clamp; keep both
                         endpoints, or the nearer one
#+END_EXAMPLE

The kept vertices come with barycentric weights =lambda=. Because each simplex
vertex remembers the two per-shape support points that formed it, blending those
by =lambda= gives the closest point on A and on B directly (=closest_a=,
=closest_b=); =distance= is =|v|=.

** From overlap to a tetrahedron for EPA

When the shapes overlap the origin lies on the simplex, which may be only a point,
edge, or triangle (the carrier need not be a full tetrahedron). [[file:epa.org][EPA]] grows that
seed into an enclosing tetrahedron before expanding, so GJK simply hands over
whatever simplex carried the origin.

** Termination

GJK stops when a probe finds no further progress (the closest point has converged,
or the support repeats a vertex), or when the origin is enclosed in a tetrahedron.
A hard iteration cap (default 32) guards against non-termination on degenerate
input. When a real stop fires, the overlap verdict is the separating-axis flag.
When instead the cap is exhausted with neither a separating direction nor a
convergence stop, nothing is proven, so GJK does *not* default to overlap: it
reports overlap only if the current simplex encloses the origin, and otherwise
reports separated (never an unproven collision). The default cap is comfortable
for every practical configuration, so this only matters to a caller who lowers it.

* Complexity

| quantity           | cost                                                   |
|--------------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| support queries    | one per iteration, default cap 32 iterations           |
| per support query  | the shape's support cost: O(N) for a convex_hull3 of N |
| per reduction      | O(1): a fixed S1D / S2D / S3D recursion                |
| memory             | O(1): a four-vertex simplex, no allocation             |

In practice GJK converges in a handful of iterations; the signed-volumes
subalgorithm shortens the search and removes the precision losses of the original
Johnson distance subalgorithm, which is why it runs measurably faster on contact.

* When to use it

- The general convex-versus-convex overlap *and distance* test, when at least one
  shape is an arbitrary polytope ([[file:convex_hull.org][convex_hull3]]) or no closed-form pair test
  fits. Any shape with a [[file:support.org][support]] overload works, and the two shapes need not be
  the same type.
- For a yes/no overlap between two analytic primitives that already have a direct
  test (sphere-sphere, AABB-AABB, sphere-AABB in [[file:intersect.org][intersect]]), prefer that test: it
  is cheaper and exact. GJK earns its keep on general hulls, mixed shapes, and
  when you need the distance or closest points.
- When you also need penetration depth and a contact normal for an overlapping
  pair (not just yes/no), hand the terminal =simplex= to [[file:epa.org][EPA]].
- =distance=, =closest_a=, and =closest_b= are meaningful only when =overlap= is
  false; the =simplex= is meaningful as EPA input only when =overlap= is true.

* Usage

#+BEGIN_SRC cpp
namespace geo = nexenne::geometry;
namespace nm = nexenne::math;

// Caller owns each shape's vertices; the hulls are views over them.
std::array<nm::vector3_f, 8> va = /* cube at the origin   */;
std::array<nm::vector3_f, 8> vb = /* cube shifted on +x   */;
geo::convex_hull3_f a{std::span<nm::vector3_f const>{va}};
geo::convex_hull3_f b{std::span<nm::vector3_f const>{vb}};

auto const r = geo::gjk(a, b, vb_center - va_center);  // seed = center to center
if (r.overlap) {
  auto const c = geo::epa(a, b, r.simplex);            // penetration depth + normal
} else {
  auto const gap = r.distance;                         // separation distance
  auto const on_a = r.closest_a, on_b = r.closest_b;   // nearest points
}
#+END_SRC
