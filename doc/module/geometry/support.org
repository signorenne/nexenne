#+TITLE: support mappings for the primitives
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil ^:nil
#+STARTUP: showall

* Purpose

=support.hpp= gives the analytic primitives the one operation GJK and EPA need
from a shape: the /support mapping/, the surface point furthest along a given
direction. [[file:convex_hull.org][convex_hull3]] already has one (a scan over its vertices); this header
adds free =support(shape, direction)= overloads for =sphere3=, =capsule3=,
=obb3=, =aabb= (3D), =triangle3=, and =segment3=, so the same collision pipeline
runs on any of those, and on any mixed pair. Each overload returns a
=vector<Real, 3>=, the exact protocol the [[file:gjk.org][convex_shape]] concept requires, and
GJK and EPA find them by argument-dependent lookup. Everything is =constexpr= and
=noexcept= and nothing allocates.

* The idea

A convex shape can be described entirely by its support mapping: hand it a
direction, it hands back the point of the shape that reaches furthest that way.
GJK and EPA never look at faces, edges, or normals; they only ever call support.
So to make a sphere or a box collide, we do not build a mesh of it, we just write
its support function in closed form. That keeps the analytic primitives analytic
(no vertex lists, no allocation) while letting them share one collision engine
with [[file:convex_hull.org][convex_hull3]].

The mapping splits into two families. Shapes with flat faces or straight edges
(box, triangle, segment, aabb) take their support at a /vertex/: a face or edge
that ties on the dot product resolves to one of its corners, so a small argmax is
exact. Rounded shapes (sphere, capsule) take their support on the /surface/: the
furthest skeleton point pushed out by the radius along the unit direction. A
capsule is just a sphere swept along a segment, so its support is the segment's
support grown by the radius, which is why the two share a shape.

* How it works

** Sphere and capsule: skeleton plus radius

The support of a ball of radius =r= centred at =c= is =c + r * dhat=, where =dhat=
is the unit search direction: the cap of the ball pointing along =d=. A capsule
generalises this: pick the spine endpoint furthest along =d=, then push it out by
=r * dhat=.

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  d ---->                       support
                                  v
     start o========o end   ->    o========O )   the far endpoint, then
            (spine)                        +r    grown by r along dhat
#+END_EXAMPLE

A (near) zero direction has no defined =dhat=, so =normalize_or= falls back to the
+x cap by convention; GJK never seeds a zero direction in practice.

** Oriented and axis-aligned boxes: pick the corner

For an =obb3= the furthest corner folds each local half-axis in by the sign of its
projection on =d=:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
  support(box, d) = center + sum over k of sign(axis_k . d) * h_k * axis_k
#+END_EXAMPLE

where =axis_k= are the columns of the box rotation (the box's local frame) and
=h_k= its half-extents. Choosing the sign per axis selects the furthest of the
eight corners without enumerating them. An =aabb= is the same with the world axes,
which reduces to picking, per component, the =max= corner when =d[k] >= 0= and the
=min= corner otherwise.

** Triangle and segment: the furthest vertex

With only three (or two) vertices the support is a plain argmax of
=dot(vertex, d)=. A segment is degenerate on its own, but it is a useful support
shape: the Minkowski sum of a segment with another convex shape models a swept
query.

** ADL is the whole extension mechanism

GJK and EPA call the unqualified =support(shape, direction)=, so adding a new
collidable shape is purely additive: write one overload in =nexenne::geometry=,
and the algorithms pick it up with no change to themselves. That is exactly how
=convex_hull3= and these primitives coexist.

* Complexity

| shape    | cost | note                                       |
|----------+------+--------------------------------------------|
| sphere   | O(1) | one normalize plus a scaled add            |
| capsule  | O(1) | one dot comparison, one normalize          |
| obb3     | O(1) | three axis rotations, three signed adds    |
| aabb     | O(1) | three sign tests                           |
| triangle | O(1) | three dot products                         |
| segment  | O(1) | two dot products                           |

Every primitive support is constant time, in contrast to the =O(N)= vertex scan
of [[file:convex_hull.org][convex_hull3]]. That is the payoff for keeping the primitives analytic.

* When to use it

- Include it whenever you want GJK or EPA to run on a =sphere3=, =obb3=,
  =capsule3=, =aabb=, =triangle3=, or =segment3=, alone or in a mixed pair.
- Prefer a dedicated closed-form test when one exists: =sphere= vs =sphere= and
  =aabb= vs =aabb= overlaps in [[file:intersect.org][intersect]] are cheaper and exact. Reach for
  support-based GJK/EPA when the pair has no closed form (capsule vs obb, hull vs
  sphere) or when you need the contact manifold EPA produces.
- The direction passed to a support need not be unit length; GJK and EPA pass raw
  search directions.

* Usage

#+BEGIN_SRC cpp
namespace geo = nexenne::geometry;
namespace nm = nexenne::math;

geo::sphere3_f const s{nm::vector3_f{0, 0, 0}, 1.0f};
geo::obb3_f const box{nm::vector3_f{1, 0, 0}, nm::vector3_f{1, 1, 1}, nm::quaternion<float>{}};

// Direct support queries (free overloads, found by ADL).
auto const cap{support(s, nm::vector3_f{1, 0, 0})};   // {1, 0, 0}, the +x cap
auto const cnr{support(box, nm::vector3_f{1, 1, 1})}; // {2, 1, 1}, a box corner

// The whole point: GJK and EPA now run on the primitives.
auto const hit{geo::gjk<float>(s, box, nm::vector3_f{1, 0, 0})};
if (hit.overlap) {
  auto const contact{geo::epa<float>(s, box, hit.simplex)};
  // contact.normal, contact.penetration_depth, contact points.
}
#+END_SRC
